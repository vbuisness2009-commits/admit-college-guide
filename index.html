Data Sources & Congressional App Challenge Disclosure

1. Official Public Data Sources

Every numerical value and policy cutoff in Admit is backed by authoritative, public-domain sources:

  • U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard: Official federal database for 25th/75th percentile SAT/ACT ranges, graduation rates, net price, and average tuition.
  • Texas Education Code § 51.803 & SB 175: Official Texas statutory code establishing the Uniform Admission Policy (Top 10% auto-admit) and UT Austin's capped auto-admit percentage.
  • Texas HB 1992 (TEC § 51.968): Texas state law capping the minimum AP exam score public universities may require at 3.
  • University Testing Services Registrars: Directly curated from official institutional credit equivalency catalogs for UT Austin, Texas A&M, UC Berkeley, University of Florida, University of Michigan, Purdue, Georgia Tech, and UIUC.

2. The Anti-Hallucination Core Principle

Commercial platforms use proprietary machine-learning formulas to output speculative "chance percentages" (e.g. "You have a 34% chance"). Because university admissions involve holistic qualitative factors (essays, interviews, recommendation letters, institutional priorities), mathematical chance calculations are unverifiable and often dangerously misleading. Admit strictly outputs verifiable facts: published score distributions, exact course waivers, and statutory rules.
